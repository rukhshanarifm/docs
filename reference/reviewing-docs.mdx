<Note>
  **This page is for the Network Nation team**, not for network managers. It is the
  process for checking a help topic is true before it goes live.
</Note>

## Why this exists

In one pass over the live app, **six discrepancies were found in five topics** — and every
one was a writing-process failure, not a product bug. The topics had been written against
the code, so their routes were right and their *steps* drifted anyway.

The most instructive finding: the single demonstrably wrong instruction was on a page
already marked approved and published. It told network managers to use a button that does
not exist on the screen.

**Approval means someone signed off. It does not mean someone clicked through it.** This
page is what makes approval mean something.

## The one rule that matters

<Card title="Click it before you write it" icon="hand-pointer">
  **Every instruction must be performed in the app, in order, before it ships.** Not
  checked against the code. Not checked against a ticket. Performed — following your own
  numbered steps as written, in the order a network manager would.
</Card>

Two failure modes this catches that nothing else does:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Controls that do not exist" icon="ghost">
    A topic described a "Reveal temporary password" button. The customer page has exactly
    two buttons: **Send invitation email** and **Login as customer**. The topic was
    approved and published.

    The code tells you a screen exists. It does not tell you what is on it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Default state" icon="eye-slash">
    Several topics described the grouping toggle as a way to *break up* a flat list. It is
    the reverse: **Group by property is the default landing view**, and **List all** is
    what produces the flat list with the columns most topics then tell you to read.

    A reader following those steps never reaches the column, because the step before it
    says to read something that is not on screen yet.

    This is why you must **start from the sidebar, not a deep link** — deep links skip the
    default state, which is exactly where this error hides.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What "performed" means

- **Start from the sidebar**, the way a network manager does.
- **Do every step**, including the ones that seem too obvious to check.
- **Read labels off the screen and paste them in.** Do not retype from memory — one topic
  said "Manual transaction" where the button says **Make a transaction**.
- **Count things rather than sampling them.** One topic listed four filter chips; there
  are nine.

## Where you must stop

The demo holds shared data. Read-only by default:

<Warning>
  **Never submit a form** — no transactions, charges or tenant moves. **Do not open an edit
  affordance on a live record** without sign-off: the pencil icons on a customer page and
  the **Continue** button in a transaction flow are both out of bounds.
</Warning>

Searching, filtering, sorting, paginating and opening detail pages are all fine.

When a step sits behind that line, **write the step and mark it unverified** rather than
guessing or omitting it. Naming an unverified step is honest; writing it as though you saw
it is not.

## Reviewer checklist

<Steps>
  <Step title="Every step performed in the app, from the sidebar, in order, as written" />
  <Step title="The default landing state of each screen is described correctly" />
  <Step title="Every UI label pasted from the screen, not retyped" />
  <Step title="Countable things counted, not sampled" />
  <Step title="Known limitations present, specific, and distinguishing absent from hidden">
    "There is no tamper detection" and "readings exist but are never shown" are different
    facts. Someone disputing a charge needs to know which one they face.
  </Step>
  <Step title="No test data reported as a product defect">
    `Test 1` and `- test` suffixes, `.invalid` emails, duplicate serials and implausible
    balances are fixtures.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Currency symbols preserved">
    The portfolio spans the UK and Nigeria. Currency is part of the fact — never normalise
    to a bare number.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Absence claims verified visually and in the page markup">
    "There is no X" is the easiest claim to get wrong by only looking at screens you
    happened to open. Check the screens a user would look on, **and** search the markup
    across every main screen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Unverified steps explicitly marked as such" />
</Steps>

## When the answer is "the platform cannot"

These pages carry the most risk and deserve the most care. State the answer in the first
sentence, in bold — **"You can't."** — then do three more things:

1. **Say why the assumption was reasonable.** The platform holds detailed meter and reading
   data, so it *looks* like the sort of system that would flag interference. It does not.
2. **Name the near-match that will mislead them.** The resident-facing app does export
   statements. Someone who hears "the platform can export statements" needs to know that is
   a different application.
3. **Give the real fallback** — a manual check, or escalation with specified details.

<Tip>
  If a topic records that an earlier draft got something wrong, **keep that note in the
  file**. A wrong answer caught once will be reintroduced by someone who does not know it
  was wrong.
</Tip>

## When the app changes

A renamed label or a moved control invalidates the topic **and** any screenshots of it.

If a limitation is lifted, **replace the topic rather than editing around it** — pages
written around an absence read incoherently once the absence is gone. And re-run the
absence checks: they are cheap, and they are the only thing that will tell you an export
button has appeared somewhere.
