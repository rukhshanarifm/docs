The words Network Manager uses, what each one actually means, and the ones that are easy
to confuse with each other.

<Note>
  **Three words do most of the damage.** **Property unit** is a *place*, **account** is a
  *bill payer*, and **customer** is a *person*. They sound interchangeable and are not.
  Most first-week confusion traces back to these three.
</Note>

## The structure everything hangs off

Every record in the platform sits somewhere on this chain:

```
Organisation  →  Client  →  Property Unit Group  →  Property Unit
                                                 →  Account
```

Read it as: *the utility operator* serves *landlords*, who own *buildings*, which contain
*flats* that are billed to *accounts*.

---

## A–Z

### Account

The **bill payer** — the financially responsible party for utility use at a unit. This is
what carries the **balance**: charges post against it, payments credit it.

Each account has a friendly identifier shown in the **Accounts** list. A resident's own
name and the account name are often not identical, which is why a search can "fail" in one
list and work in another.

When a unit is empty it is still held by an account — a **landlord account** — so costs
during a vacancy remain attributable to someone.

### Auto deduct

A setting that takes a **non-commodity charge** automatically from a resident's balance
when it falls due, instead of waiting for them to pay it.

When it fires it takes the **full amount regardless of the balance**, so it can push an
account into debt. That is deliberate: the alternative would leave money owed but
invisible.

It can be set from either side, and the two are not the same thing:

| Who sets it | What happens at a change of tenancy |
|---|---|
| **You**, on the charge | Applies as a term of the charge |
| **The resident**, on their account | Ends with their tenancy — it does not transfer |

A resident's consent ending with their tenancy is intentional: inheriting it would mean
drawing money from a new resident's balance on someone else's decision.

### Balance

What a resident owes or is owed, shown in the **Accounts** list once you click
**List all**. Negative means they owe money; positive means they are in credit.

It is current to the **last meter reading**, not the last bill. It already includes usage
that has been read but not yet formally billed, and payments still clearing — so it is
ahead of the billing run, not behind it.

### Client

The landlord or customer organisation your organisation serves. A client owns buildings
and the **tariffs** used within them.

**One organisation has many clients.** Never assume one client per organisation.

### Commodity

What is being measured and billed: **electricity**, **water**, **heat** or **gas**.
Commodity lives on meters, billing points and tariffs.

### Customer

A **person** — a human being who lives at a unit. Customers appear under **Customers**
and log into the separate resident-facing app.

**One account can have more than one customer on it** — a couple sharing a flat, for
example. One is the primary holder; others are additional holders.

### End user

Another name for a **customer** or resident. The term distinguishes them from network
managers: the two are entirely separate user types, with separate logins and separate
applications.

### Network *(retired)*

**"Network" no longer means anything in the platform.** It used to be a grouping inside an
organisation; its job was split between the **commodity** and the hierarchy above. There
is no network entity.

Avoid the word in training and support conversations. Someone asking "how do I create a
new network?" almost always means a new **property unit group**.

It survives in two names only: *network manager* (the role) and *network balance* (a
property unit group's ledger).

### Network manager

**You** — the administrator who runs a utility network day to day.

Access is **scoped**, and two network managers at the same client can legitimately see
different things. Scope can be set at the level of the whole organisation, a single
client, a single property unit group, or a single property unit. Everything you can see is
already filtered to your scope, which is why lists span all your facilities without you
choosing one first.

Scope controls **what you can see**, not **what you can do to it**. Within your own scope
there is no seniority tier.

### Non-commodity charge

A charge that is not metered utility usage — rent, a service charge, security, gardening,
waste. These are the charges **auto deduct** applies to.

### Organisation

The utility operator itself, and the top-level entity. It sets the **currency**, the
**timezone**, the **branding** and the payment provider.

The timezone matters more than it sounds: all dates, charge boundaries and due times are
interpreted in the organisation's timezone, never your browser's. A colleague in a
different country sees the same times you do.

### Property unit

A **place** — the individual flat, house or communal space. It can also be a generation
site or a substation.

A property unit has **one account at a time**, but many accounts over its lifetime, as
residents come and go.

### Property unit group

A **building or site**: the units that share an address. It owns the accounts and any
building-level bulk meters.

This is the most-renamed concept in conversation. All of these mean the same thing:

- Site
- Facility
- Property
- Building
- Property unit group *(the app's own name)*

If someone says "add this to the site", they mean the property unit group.

### Register

A counter on a meter. One meter can have several — which is why a statement's line items
and a meter's registers line up with each other.

### Superuser

A network manager with implicit access to everything, across all organisations. A small
number of actions are restricted to superusers — most visibly the **Users** section.

**If you do not see Users in the sidebar, you are not a superuser.** That is normal; most
network managers are not.

### Tariff

The rates a billing point is charged at. Tariffs belong to a **client**.

## Words that are easy to mix up

| These sound alike | But |
|---|---|
| Account / Customer | A bill payer vs. a person. One account can have several customers |
| Property unit / Property unit group | A flat vs. the building it sits in |
| Network / Network manager | The first is retired; the second is your role |
| Balance / Cleared balance | Only one figure is shown, and it includes payments still clearing |
| Non-commodity charge / Metered usage | Fees and rent vs. what a meter measured |
